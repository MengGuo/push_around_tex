\begin{abstract}
The passage of large vehicles in cluttered environments is often blocked by
movable obstacles, motivating the use of mobile robot teams to proactively
clear traversable corridors. Existing approaches to Navigation Among Movable
Obstacles (NAMO) mainly plan abstract sequences of obstacle displacements but
often neglect physical feasibility, overlooking robot dimensions, obstacle mass,
contact geometry, and required forces, which can lead to strategies that fail
in execution.
This work introduces \emph{PushAround}, a physics-informed hybrid search
framework that incrementally constructs executable clearing plans by coupling
geometric connectivity reasoning with short-horizon simulation. Repeated
W--clearance connectivity graph (WCCG) queries expose the frontier gaps where the
corridor must be widened and rank them by estimated downstream costs, while the
search generates direct or recursive pushing candidates -- which obstacles to
displace, in which directions, with which contacts and forces -- admitting only
physics-validated candidates.
\change{
Numerical experiments validate WCCG against standard connectivity checks. 
PushAround further scales to denser clutter and teams of up to four robots, 
while remaining robust to simulated execution-side physical-parameter mismatch.
}
\end{abstract}
