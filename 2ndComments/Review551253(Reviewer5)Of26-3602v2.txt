I thank the authors for their careful revision and detailed responses
to the reviewers’ comments. This is a substantial revision, and I think
the paper has improved considerably. The additional experiments on
physics-model uncertainty, obstacle density, and larger robot teams
provide stronger support for the proposed method. The explanations of
the WCCG connectivity check, the search guarantees, and the hardware
execution are also clearer now.

Overall, I think the authors have addressed the main concerns raised in
the first round. The combination of geometric reasoning, collaborative
pushing, and physics-informed search is interesting, and the simulation
and hardware results support the contribution. I have a few remaining
minor comments that could improve the clarity and consistency of the
paper:

1.The response explains that the density experiments use a precomputed
mode table, while the mismatch and robot-team experiments use greedy
mode optimization. It also mentions a different scene realization for
the two-robot team-scaling experiment. I suggest including these
details in Sec. IV-A, since they help explain the different planning
times reported for M=30 and N=2. Please also clarify whether the time
needed to prepare the precomputed mode table is included in the
reported planning time.

2.Sec. III-C3 states that a physics rollout may be bypassed when a mode
has been validated before and the swept region is collision-free. A
brief explanation of the conditions for reusing this validation would
be helpful. In particular, please clarify which state and physical
parameters must remain unchanged, and how the successor state and costs
are obtained when the rollout is skipped.

3.The new mismatch experiments are useful. However, the perturbed
trials reuse ten base seeds and change one parameter family at a time,
while the baselines use five seeds per condition. The response
acknowledges these limitations and the differences in nominal success
rates. I suggest adding a short qualification in the manuscript so that
readers can interpret the robustness comparisons appropriately.

4.In Sec. IV-B2, the text describes “15.9 s of deviations” due to force
limits, drifting, and slippage, whereas Fig. 11 reports execution time
as 77.1 ± 15.9 s. Please clarify whether 15.9 s is the standard
deviation or a separately measured recovery time. There is also a small
inconsistency in the response: the text on p. 4 gives 159.6 s as the
mean execution time under ±40% perturbations, while Table R2 gives
115.0 s for the aggregate and 159.6 s for the force-limit ×0.6
condition.

5.Sec. IV-B1 says that replanning is triggered “only” when the position
or orientation error exceeds the thresholds. Sec. III-E1 also lists
failed contacts, stalled pushing, and invalidated connectivity. Please
make these descriptions consistent.

These are minor comments on clarity and reporting. I am satisfied with
the overall revision and recommend acceptance.
