Dear Professor Asfour,

On behalf of my co-authors, I am pleased to resubmit the revised version of our
manuscript entitled "PushAround: Collaborative Path Clearing via Physics-Informed
Hybrid Search" for possible publication as a regular paper in IEEE Robotics and
Automation Letters.

We thank the Associate Editor and both reviewers for the constructive
evaluation. The reviewers recognized the contribution of the paper and raised two
formal weaknesses together with a set of clarification requests; the revision
addresses every one of them, without changing the scope of the method. The main
additions are:

1. A dedicated planner-execution physics-mismatch study. Planning always uses
   the nominal model while only the execution simulator is perturbed, one
   parameter family at a time (obstacle mass, lateral friction, spinning
   friction and robot force limit) by factors covering +/-20% and +/-40%. Across
   170 trials PushAround succeeds in 166 (97.6%, and 97.5% at the largest
   perturbations), whereas under the same +/-40% perturbations the SL-Push (sim)
   and DFS-WCCG baselines reach only 32.5% and 57.5%.

2. Strengthened statistics for the dense-clutter evaluation. The obstacle-density
   sweep now uses ten independent seed sequences per density instead of five, and
   reports dispersion and Wilson confidence intervals. PushAround keeps a 100%
   success rate for all tested densities from 15 to 45 movable obstacles at a mean
   planning time of 2.5-53.4 s, while all baselines degrade to 0% at the highest
   density.

3. A robot-team scaling study together with the two mode-generation refinements
   it motivated (bounded backtracking contact assignment and a temporary
   per-task robot release). Because these refinements belong to the shared mode
   generator, all compared methods were re-run on the same code revision to keep
   the comparison on equal footing. PushAround solves all ten
   trials at two, three and four robots, with the lowest execution time and the
   fewest pushes among the compared methods.

4. A WCCG connectivity validation against both a continuous configuration-space
   reference and a rasterized reference over 300 random scenes, including
   non-convex obstacles, showing agreement with the continuous reference in all
   300 scenes with zero false positives and zero false negatives.

5. Clarifications of the mathematical and implementation interfaces requested by
   the reviewers: Eq. (5) now defines the hybrid schedule and Eq. (6) its realized
   clearance cost, which is accumulated in the node priority of Eq. (14), with no
   optimality or completeness claim, the meaning of the clearance
   duration and the manipulation effort is stated explicitly, the optimized
   contact forces are declared feasibility variables rather than hardware force
   setpoints, coupled object motion is explained as an outcome of the joint
   rigid-body rollout, and the execution monitor together with the shared
   comparison protocol (timeout, control-step budget and reachability checks) is
   specified.

A detailed point-by-point response to the Associate Editor and to Reviewers 3 and
4 accompanies this resubmission. All changes in the manuscript are highlighted in
blue in "root.pdf"; "root_clean.pdf" is the same manuscript without the
highlighting. Supplementary videos illustrating the planning, simulation and
hardware execution are provided.

The manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under
consideration elsewhere. All authors have approved the resubmission.

We thank you very much for the handling of our paper and are looking forward to
hearing from you.
